The read-out

What this run shows, and what it can’t

Findings

  • gpt-6-astra ranks first on the composite (0.97).
  • claude-opus-5-5 has the highest raw reply quality (4.94/5) but the slowest median latency (10.9s vs 6.0-6.8s).
  • claude-opus-5-5 has the best quality on 5 of 6 ticket topics.
  • Two judges from different vendors agree on the order (Spearman rho 1.00, 116 answers).
  • Quality on 20 held-out tickets stays within 0.04 of the public suite.
  • 2 confirmed flags across 180 graded answers (gemini-4-argon: 2); the reply contradicted its own declared actions.

Limits

  • 40 public tickets plus 20 held-out, from one reference workload (support triage). It may not transfer to your tickets, which is why the tool is self-hosted.
  • Every candidate ran at reasoning effort low.
  • Cost is not scored in this run.
  • Latency for CLI candidates includes agent-CLI startup, so it is pipeline time, not model speed.
  • Judged by Claude Code CLI (claude-opus-5), cross-checked by Codex CLI (gpt-6-astra).
  • A flag counts only when both judges raise it. The primary judge flagged 4 answers, the second 10, and 2 were flagged by both, so flag counts depend on which judge you ask.
  • The primary judge (claude-opus-5) is from the same vendor as candidate claude-opus-5-5. The second judge (gpt-6-astra) is also a candidate. Self-preference is possible, which is why a second vendor cross-checks the first judge.

Why the number is trustworthy

Four rules the scoring never breaks

i.

Graded exactly, where exactness is possible

Routing, priority and declared actions are checked against gold labels. No judge opinion where there is a right answer.

ii.

Judged only where judgment is required

Policy adherence, resolution and tone are scored by an LLM judge against the exact policy the candidate saw.

iii.

Every ranking is cross-checked by a second, different-vendor judge

We report the rank correlation between the two — a ranking that's only defensible to its own vendor's model gets caught.

iv.

A violation is pass/fail, never a discount

critical_violation is a hard gate. A forbidden refund promise or a leaked prompt fails outright.

The gap in procurement today

What a vendor benchmark can't tell you

Benchmarks are marketing.

None are scored against your policy, your tickets or your definition of a good answer.

A model swap can break production.

Quality drops and nobody notices until a customer complains. No gate caught it first.

The failure that matters goes untested.

A cheaper model that leaks its system prompt is a liability with a lower price.

$/1M tokens is not a budget line.

Nobody turns the rate card into $/month at your volume before signing.

What it's for

Four decisions, not one demo

Vendor selection

Your shortlist against every other flagship on the same tickets, ranked by quality, latency and cost weighted to your workload.

scorecard.weights: quality / latency / cost

Safety red-teaming

Injection, prompt-leak and over-refusal traps ride along in every run. A violation is a hard gate, never averaged in.

critical_violation → hard gate

Cost governance

Set monthly ticket volume once. Every rate card becomes a $/month figure finance can act on.

scorecard.monthly_volume → $/month

Regression protection

Run a new model version against a saved baseline. The gate fails the deploy if quality drops or violations rise.

--baseline / --regression-threshold → exit 1
